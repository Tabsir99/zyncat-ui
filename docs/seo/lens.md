# lens - keyword research

Primary: image zoom (KD 7, >100, ranks for: w3schools/howto_js_image_zoom #8 / 150 mo)
Secondary: react image zoom (KD 3), image zoom on hover (KD 3), image magnifier (KD 47, magnify.so #7 / 70 mo), magnify image on hover (KD 11, webflow #3 / 60 mo)

## Does "lens" survive in the title? No - and this one is not close.

Every test says the word earns nothing here.

- `generator lens component` returned **one** row: `lens component` itself. There is no
  web-development demand behind the word at all.
- `generator lens effect` is 100% photography: `fisheye lens effect`, `anamorphic lens
effect`, `tilt shift lens effect`, `telephoto lens effect`, `petzval`, `fresnel`, `50mm`.
- `generator zoom lens` is 100% camera bodies: `canon zoom lens`, `sony e pz 16-50mm`,
  `nikon zoom lens`, `what is a zoom lens` (>1K), `does iphone air have telephoto zoom
lens?`. Bare `lens` adds contact lenses on top of that.
- The decisive one: **`ui.aceternity.com/components/lens` earns 0 traffic and has zero
  ranking keywords.** Aceternity UI ships the same component under the same name on a
  domain pulling 8.3K/mo, and its Lens page is invisible. If a DR-heavy design system
  cannot rank a page called "Lens", the word is not the problem to solve - it is the
  problem to route around.

This is a harder call than the odometer one, and it lands the other way. There, `react
odometer`, `odometer animation`, `odometer effect` and `odometer css` were real
zero-competition clusters worth free traffic, so the word stayed - late and never bare.
Here the equivalent clusters do not exist: `react zoom` is polluted by Nike React Zoom
trainers and Zoom-the-videoconferencing-app chat reactions, and `lens component` is a
single dead row. Keeping "Lens" in the title would spend characters on a camera-lens
misread and buy nothing.

So the title drops it entirely: **`React Image Zoom Magnifier on Hover`** (35 chars). The
URL is `/lens` and the H1 is `Lens`, so anyone who has seen the component still finds it
by name; `react lens component` sits last in `keywords` for that recall. The lede opens on
"An image zoom on hover" and lets the H1 above it supply the name.

Why the rest of the title is what it is. `image zoom` leads because it is the primary and
because `react image zoom` is a proven keyword contested by seven separate weak pages.
`Magnifier` follows rather than `Image Magnifier` because bare `image magnifier` is KD 47 -
the SERP above magnify.so is AI photo-enlarger money (imageenlarger.com, photoaid, picsart),
a different intent we would lose and would not want. `on Hover` beats `Component` on
evidence: the hover modifier carries ~340/mo of proven competitor traffic across
`hover image zoom` (80), `zoom on hover` (70), `magnify image on hover` (60), `image zoom
on hover` (50), `image hover zoom effect css` (50), `image zoom on hover css` (30), while
`component` phrasings all return `<100` with no measured difficulty.

**The 700/mo prize is unclaimed.** `zoom image` sends 700 visits a month to a w3schools
page sitting at **#11** - page two. That page is the 2015-era vanilla-JS "image magnifier
glass" tutorial, and it earns 37/mo in total. The intent behind it (a circular glass that
follows the cursor) is exactly this component.

react-inner-image-zoom #2, react-image-magnify #3, animate-ui #4, malaman #6,
react-zoom-pan-pinch #9, innerimagezoom #3 - and the whole keyword is KD **3**. Nobody owns
it; five of the seven pages earn under 40/mo.

WooCommerce Zoom Magnifier and the WooCommerce/Shopify/Magento plugin queries are the
paying intent, and the generator returned that whole surface (`woocommerce zoom magnifier`,
`shopify zoom product image`, `magento 2 product zoom`, `product zoom jquery`). The FAQ
names the incumbents rather than pretending they do not exist.

## Notes on what the FAQ may claim

Every FAQ answer is grounded in `src/components/expressive/lens/Lens.tsx`,
`src/components/expressive/lens/lens.css`, `src/engine/loop.ts` or `README.md`:

- `children`, `magnification`, `radius`, `chromatic`, `className`, `style`, `htmlProps` are
  the whole prop surface. There is no `zoomImage`/`largeImage` prop, no lens-shape prop and
  no trigger prop, so the FAQ says "there is no second image URL prop" rather than
  inventing one.
- `magnification` clamped 1.2-6 (`MAGNIFICATION_MIN`/`MAGNIFICATION_MAX`) and `radius`
  clamped 60-260 px (`RADIUS_MIN`/`RADIUS_MAX`), both quoted exactly. `radius` is the glass
  radius, not its diameter - the FAQ says so.
- "Clones its children into the glass" is `cloneInto`, called for both `parts.core` and
  `parts.rim`; the clone gets `aria-hidden="true"` and `.lens__copy`.
- "A MutationObserver re-clones" is the `MutationObserver` on `parts.content` with
  `childList`, `subtree`, `characterData` and `attributes`, throttled by
  `RECLONE_INTERVAL_MS = 90`.
- "Re-rasterised as vector type at the larger size" is the usage doc's own claim in
  `Lens.usage.md`, produced by CSS `scale` on real DOM rather than a canvas readback.
- The photo caveat in the quality answer is the honest consequence of that: the clone is the
  same `<img>` element, so the magnified detail is bounded by the source file's natural
  resolution. Nothing in the component fetches a larger image.
- "A specular highlight that trails the direction of travel" is
  `const trailing = optics.angle + Math.PI` driving `parts.specular`; "chromatic fringing
  that strengthens with speed" is `FRINGE_BASE + speed * FRINGE_SPEED`, and
  `chromatic={false}` sets that opacity to `'0'`.
- "Follows the pointer with no lag" is `optics.centreX = optics.pointerX` each frame with no
  easing on position - only `magnification`, `opacity` and `press` are eased.
- Arrow keys (`KEYBOARD_STEP = 24`) and `Escape` are the `keydown` handler; the stage is
  `tabIndex={0}` and `blur` disengages.
- Accessibility: `{children}` render inside `.lens__content` in the normal tree; only the
  magnified copies carry `aria-hidden`.
- SSR: `{children}` are in the server-rendered HTML; the glass layers are empty
  `aria-hidden` divs until the effect runs, so the "server-rendered, glass built after
  hydration" wording matches the component exactly.
- Reduced motion: `loop` returns early when `UIMotion.reduced`, calling `snap` once and
  never starting a rAF; `Lens` calls `settleNow()` on every interaction in that mode, so the
  glass still works and simply does not ease - which is what the FAQ says.
- Off-screen and hidden-tab pausing is `loop`'s `IntersectionObserver` plus the
  `visibilitychange` listener; `Lens` passes `el: parts.stage`, so both apply.
- React 19, compiled ESM with `'use client'` intact, no `transpilePackages`, zero runtime
  dependencies: `README.md`.
- The competitor names in the "best tool" answer are the pages the SERP and traffic data
  actually returned; the answer describes only this component's API, not theirs.
